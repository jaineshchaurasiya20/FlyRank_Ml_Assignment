0. Abstract
Modern search engines continuously recalibrate organic rankings, causing mature website content to experience silent traffic decay that editorial teams typically detect months too late. Working with the FlyRank AI Search Intelligence dataset spanning 79M+ warehouse event rows and a 30,000-page cross-client benchmark across 32 enterprise domains, we evaluate whether machine learning can prioritize recoverable content decay more effectively than heuristic rules. We trained and audited a Gradient Boosted Decision Tree (GBDT) pipeline under a strict client-holdout validation protocol, explicitly eliminating label leakage and domain-identity memorization. On unseen client domains with a 39.1% decay base rate, the model achieves a Precision@50 of 86.0% and a PR-AUC of 0.6772, significantly outperforming the production heuristic rule baseline (Precision@50 = 22.0%, PR-AUC = 0.4700). We operationalize these predictive rankings into a human-in-the-loop Content Action Playbook that maps multi-dimensional search signals into concrete editorial workflows with clear cost-benefit economics, safety guardrails, and retrain tripwires.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Content marketing and organic search optimization represent substantial corporate capital expenditures. However, once published, organic search equity degrades dynamically over time: search engine algorithms update ranking weights, competitors publish fresher material, and searcher intent shifts. Most enterprise organizations operate reactively—noticing traffic loss only after high-performing pages fall off Page 1 of Google Search results.

Editorial teams face an acute triage bottleneck: out of thousands of URLs across large publishing libraries, which specific pages should human writers and SEO specialists prioritize for updates each sprint?

Today, most SEO platforms rely on transparent but rigid heuristic rules—flagging pages solely based on days since publication, flat CTR thresholds, or gross volume. In practice, these static rules suffer from excessive false alarm rates, squandering expensive editorial bandwidth on stable URLs while missing fast-eroding pages. This paper frames content refresh prioritization as a supervised machine learning ranking problem: predicting 30-day forward traffic decay from backward-looking search behavior, and translating those scores into an operational editorial playbook.

2. Data Contract, Provenance & Exclusions

Our experiments leverage FlyRank's pseudonymized enterprise search intelligence warehouse. All data originates from real daily synchronizations of Google Search Console (GSC) and Google Analytics 4 (GA4) ingested into BigQuery and exported under strict anonymization contracts.

Data Safety & Leakage Guard: Because is_declining_label is derived mathematically from trend_direction, which is computed from trend_pct = (impressions_last_30d - impressions_prev_30d) / impressions_prev_30d, both trend_direction and trend_pct were strictly excluded from model feature sets. Furthermore, client IDs and content IDs were restricted to partition keys only, and FlyRank product flags (health_score, quick_win) were excluded to avoid circular learning.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

To ensure model performance generalizes to real customer deployments, we implemented an honest validation protocol designed to eliminate domain-identity memorization and feature leakage.

Honest Client-Holdout Partitioning

Standard random row-level splitting is invalid for enterprise SEO data: URLs from the same domain share domain authority, backlink profiles, and technical site templates. Random splits allow models to "memorize" client domain traits across folds. We enforced a strict Client-Holdout Partition:

  • Training Set: 26 client domains (27,675 rows, 55.5% decay base rate).
  • Sealed Test Set: 6 unseen client domains (2,325 rows, 39.1% decay base rate).

Production Feature Pipeline

We engineered 7 strictly backward-looking features capturing search visibility, trajectory momentum, and visitor engagement:

Feature Name Formula / Source Rationale & Nuance Handled
clicks_last_30d GSC clicks in trailing 30d Captures real traffic volume and high-value conversion opportunity.
impressions_last_30d GSC impressions in trailing 30d Baseline search demand surface area; separates large pillars from niche pages.
decay_ratio_lag (imp_last_30d + 1) / (imp_prev_30d + 1) Lagged volume velocity ratio without touching forward label metrics.
position_clean avg_position (0 imputed to 50.0) Corrects GSC's 0-value ("no data") trap to avoid treating unranked as rank 0.
ctr GSC clicks / impressions × 100 Direct indicator of snippet relevance and SERP CTR deficits.
sessions_last_30d GA4 sessions in trailing 30d Multi-system verification connecting search impressions to onsite visits.
bounce_proxy 100.0 - engagement_rate GA4 engagement rate inversion; proxies page dissatisfaction.
Figure 1: Validation Split Audit. Evaluating on a naive random row split yields an inflated PR-AUC of 0.7821. Under an honest client-holdout split, PR-AUC drops to 0.6453 (Random Forest) and 0.6772 (GBDT). The 0.1368 generalization gap quantifies the extent of domain-identity memorization.

Key Observed Findings

  1. Substantial Top-K Precision Gain: For an editorial team reviewing 50 URLs per cycle, the machine learning model ensures 43 out of 50 URLs are genuine decay cases, compared to only 11 out of 50 under the heuristic rule.
  2. Non-Linear Discrimination: GBDT outperforms linear models (PR-AUC 0.6772 vs 0.5280), proving that search decay signals involve complex threshold interactions between rank position and impression volume.
  3. Signal Importance Hierarchy: Permutation importance identifies decay_ratio_lag (38.4%), impressions_last_30d (24.1%), and position_clean (16.7%) as the dominant drivers of decay prediction.

5. Limitations & Honest Claim Framing

In keeping with FlyRank's house research discipline, we state the empirical boundaries of this work explicitly. We use safe claim vocabulary: observed, measured, directional, and decision-support.

Non-Causal Assurance: This model measures observational association, not causal mechanics. Flagging a URL does not guarantee that executing an editorial refresh will produce a deterministic ranking increase. Rankings depend on external competitor actions, crawl schedules, and broad SERP algorithm recalibrations.

Known Operational Boundaries

  • Low-Volume Signal Breakdown: For pages with <20 impressions per 30-day window, stochastic query variance dominates signal. We deliberately truncate the bottom 29.6% of low-volume catalog URLs from automated triage.
  • Catalog Domain Calibration: The dataset reflects 32 B2B SaaS and content publishing websites. The model is uncalibrated for seasonal e-commerce catalogs (e.g. Black Friday retail promotions) where traffic drops reflect calendar shifts rather than content decay.
  • Cold-Start Exclusion: Newly published content items (<90 days old) are excluded because initial search indexing dynamics exhibit high volatility unsuited to mature decay modeling.

6. Ranked Recommendations (Content Action Playbook)

A machine learning probability score $\hat{y} \in [0, 1]$ cannot edit an article. To deliver operational utility, we map model probabilities and search performance attributes into an automated Content Action Playbook.

Action Priority Scoring

Queue priority is determined by weighting decay probability with total search visibility:

Action Priority Score = Decay Probability × (0.5 + 0.5 × [ln(1 + impressions_30d) / max ln(1 + impressions_30d)])

Strict No-Go Automation Policies

The playbook explicitly defines actions that must NEVER be fully automated:

  • Autonomous Direct Publishing: No LLM-generated revision should publish directly to client CMS without human editorial signoff.
  • Automated 410 / Deletion: Content pruning must be human-verified to protect inbound backlink equity.
  • Comparison / Alternative Pages: Commercial comparison articles involve sensitive legal and brand claims requiring mandatory editorial oversight.
